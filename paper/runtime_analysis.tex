\paragraph{Runtime measurement caveat.}
The runtimes in Table~\ref{tab:runtime} and in the accompanying bar chart
are single wall-clock measurements taken on a shared, non-isolated
machine, and should be read as order-of-magnitude estimates rather than
precise figures. Three sources of uncertainty dominate. First, the
values bundle a per-epoch cost with the number of epochs actually run,
and early stopping halts different runs at different epochs (between 6
and 25 for the reinforcement-learning models), so a lower runtime can
reflect earlier convergence rather than faster computation; normalizing
to per-epoch cost removes this confound. Second, wall-clock time on a
loaded machine varies with background contention. Third, sub-second
measurements are dominated by one-time warm-up costs such as the first
construction of the autograd graph. The magnitude of these effects is
visible in the two models we re-ran across ten seeds: the single-run
figure for PennyLane~QDPG (9.10\,s) underestimates the ten-seed mean
(17.79\,s) by roughly a factor of two, whereas the single-run figure for
Classical~DDPG (3.60\,s) overestimates its ten-seed mean (0.23\,s) by
roughly a factor of fifteen, the latter attributable to cold-start
overhead absorbed by the first measured run. We therefore claim only the
order-of-magnitude structure of the results---classical
$\ll$ PennyLane $\ll$ Qiskit, spanning two to three orders of
magnitude---which is far larger than the measurement scatter and is
robust to all three effects above. A controlled re-timing (repeated runs
with a fixed epoch budget on an isolated machine) is left to future work
and would be required before any finer-grained runtime claim.
